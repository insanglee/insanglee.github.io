Exploring Sustainable Wellbeing with the Happy Planet Index

Authors

Minyeong Yoon

Insang Lee

Published

October 23, 2026

Project Overview

This project uses the Happy Planet Index 2026 dataset to explore the relationship between economic wealth, subjective wellbeing, ecological impact, and sustainable wellbeing across countries.

Rather than treating GDP alone as an indicator of national wellbeing, we use three complementary visualizations to examine how GDP per capita relates to the Happy Planet Index (HPI), life satisfaction, and ecological footprint.

The interactive application contains three visualizations:

  1. Chart 9: Interactive Scatter Plot — GDP per capita and Happy Planet Index
  2. Chart 18: Interactive Bubble Chart — GDP per capita, life satisfaction, and ecological footprint
  3. Contour Plot — distribution and concentration of countries across GDP per capita and HPI

All visualizations use 2025 observations from the Happy Planet Index 2026 public dataset.

Research Question

How does economic wealth relate to sustainable wellbeing across countries?

The three visualizations examine this broader question from different perspectives.

  • Does higher GDP per capita correspond to a higher Happy Planet Index?
  • How do life satisfaction and ecological footprint vary across levels of economic wealth?
  • Where are countries concentrated within the joint distribution of GDP and HPI?

Together, these charts provide a multidimensional view of sustainable wellbeing that cannot be captured by GDP alone.

Data

Data source: Happy Planet Index 2026 public dataset.

The analysis uses country-level observations for 2025.

Key variables include:

Variable Description
GDP per capita GDP per capita in PPP-adjusted constant 2021 international dollars
Happy Planet Index Measure of sustainable wellbeing
Life satisfaction Self-reported life satisfaction
Ecological footprint Environmental resource use
Continent Geographic region of each country

For the 2025 observations, the GDP measure uses the dataset variable reporting 2024 GDP per capita for the 2025 HPI observations.

The application below was developed in R Shiny and published through Posit Connect Cloud.

The controls allow users to interactively explore differences across countries and compare patterns across the three visualizations.

Chart 9: GDP and Happy Planet Index

How Does GDP Relate to HPI?

The first visualization is an interactive scatter plot corresponding to Chart 9 in Abela’s Chart Chooser.

A scatter plot is appropriate because both GDP per capita and HPI are numeric variables, and the primary purpose is to examine their relationship across countries.

  • X-axis: GDP per capita
  • Y-axis: Happy Planet Index
  • Color: Continent
  • Observation: Country

GDP per capita is displayed on a logarithmic scale to make countries across very different income levels easier to compare.

Interactive features include:

  • GDP per capita range filter
  • Continent filter
  • Country-name labels
  • Optional trend line
  • Hover information
  • Plotly zoom and pan controls

The visualization allows users to investigate whether greater economic wealth is consistently associated with higher levels of sustainable wellbeing and whether patterns differ across geographic regions.

Chart 18: Wealth, Wellbeing, and Ecological Footprint

The second visualization is an interactive bubble chart corresponding to Chart 18 in Abela’s Chart Chooser.

A bubble chart is appropriate because the visualization examines the relationship among three quantitative variables simultaneously.

  • X-axis: GDP per capita
  • Y-axis: Life satisfaction
  • Bubble size / color: Ecological footprint

Each bubble represents a country.

This visualization extends the GDP comparison by showing that countries with similar levels of economic wealth or life satisfaction may differ substantially in their ecological footprint.

Interactive controls allow users to modify bubble size, GDP scaling, labels, and hover information.

Additional Visualization: Contour Plot

The third visualization is a contour plot examining the joint distribution of GDP per capita and the Happy Planet Index.

Unlike the individual-country emphasis of the scatter plot, the contour visualization emphasizes where countries are concentrated within the GDP–HPI distribution.

The contour lines identify areas with greater concentrations of observations, while optional country points and a trend line provide additional context.

Interactive controls allow users to adjust:

  • Number of contour levels
  • Logarithmic GDP scaling
  • Country points
  • Country labels
  • Trend line

This visualization complements the scatter and bubble charts by emphasizing the overall distributional structure of the data rather than only individual observations.

Shared Reactivity

To support direct comparison across visualizations, the Scatter Plot and Bubble Chart share the same GDP range filter.

A single reactive dataset is generated from the selected GDP range and is supplied to both charts.

Therefore, when the GDP range changes, both visualizations update using the same subset of countries.

The Contour Plot retains its own independent controls.

This shared reactive structure ensures that comparisons between the Scatter and Bubble charts remain consistent while allowing the Contour Plot to serve its separate visualization purpose.
